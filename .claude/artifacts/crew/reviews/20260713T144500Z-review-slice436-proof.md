## 1. Is the blinding real? — Yes, and honestly scoped

The judge (`lib/judge.ts`) only ever receives `{type, text}` atom pairs — it was **already** structurally blind to model/config identity before this slice, independent of any harness-level blinding. This script's *own* contribution is blinding its bookkeeping: configs get a random letter (`shuffled()` uses `Math.random`, verified), every console line and `raw-blinded-results.json` entry during the run use only the letter, and the `letterToConfig` map is never printed until `revealLetterMapping` runs at the end. Confirmed by reading `run.log`: per-fixture lines during the run print only `[config A/B/C/D]`, never the label. The report's own wording ("structurally blind... not a discipline promise") is accurate, not oversold.

One caveat not mentioned anywhere: the per-fixture console lines during the live run print atom/relation counts and latency alongside the letter, and codex vs. qwen have visibly different behavioral signatures (codex: faster, non-zero relations; qwen: slower, near-zero relations). A human watching stdout live could infer identity from this fingerprint well before the reveal, even though the letter itself is never printed. Not a fabricated blinding claim — just an unstated limit on what the blinding actually protects against (protects the judge and the final write-up; does not protect a human's live impression while watching the run scroll by). Worth a one-line disclosure, not a blocker.

## 2. Is F1 computed correctly? — Yes, with one wording overclaim

`precision = (extracted.length - junk.length) / extracted.length` in `judge.ts:160` — this counts `matches_gold` **and** `reasonable_extra` atoms both as "precision-correct," only `junk` is penalized. That's a legitimate and disclosed design (precision measures "not hallucinated/trivial," not "matches a specific gold atom") — the report doesn't claim otherwise. `recall` is straightforward (gold-matched / gold-total). F1 is the harmonic mean of the **run-aggregate** avgRecall/avgPrecision (macro-style), not a mean of per-fixture F1s — standard, undisclosed but not misleading.

The **relations** count, however, is pure quantity — there is no gold-relation ground truth anywhere in `fixtures.ts`'s `GoldAtom` type, and `filterAtomRelations` (05-extract.ts) only guards against structurally hallucinated triples (subject/object not in this atom's own entities[]), never semantic truth. The report's line "**codex-subscription extracts relations far more reliably than qwen**" (TL;DR and body, x2) reads as a correctness claim the harness cannot support — nothing here checks whether any given relation is actually true. Recommend "extracts relations far more *frequently/consistently*" instead of "reliably." **[MINOR]**
