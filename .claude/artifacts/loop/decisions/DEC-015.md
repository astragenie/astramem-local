# DEC-015 — FEAT-479 is the sole owner of the extraction-prompt surface

## Decision

All work on `src/distill/prompts/extract.ts` (and the prompt-selection seam in
`src/distill/stages/05-extract.ts`) is consolidated under **FEAT-479** as the
single canonical ticket, with ONE builder-owner at any time.

Folded into FEAT-479 scope:
- Master-plan W5 (richer memories: 300–800 chars, claim + rationale +
  consequence + provenance; AtomSchema text cap 500 → 1000)
- Conversational-profile plan S2 (`distill.extractProfile:
  'engineering' | 'conversational'` prompt variant + per-ingest override)
- Type-conditioned richness rule (rich shape for lesson/decision, atomic for
  fact/command — stated explicitly in the prompt, per review MED-3)
- FEAT-481's relation-triples PROMPT slice (the triples output-schema addition
  rides FEAT-479's builder; FEAT-481 keeps the storage/wire/MCP slices and is
  a DEPENDENT of FEAT-479, not a co-owner of the prompt file)

## Why

Four efforts were converging on the same file from different plans
(master-plan W5, conversational plan, memory-quality design §3.2, FEAT-481).
Prose-level "should merge" notes in three documents provided no mechanism —
a triage pass could open a fifth redundant ticket, and two builders on
extract.ts is a guaranteed collision (constitution: scope discipline).

## How to apply

- Triage/intake: do NOT open new FEATs touching extract.ts; route additions
  as FEAT-479 scope amendments.
- The parallel session's master-plan review outcome amends FEAT-479 scope,
  not a new ticket.
- FEAT-481 slices order: its extraction-prompt slice is blocked-by FEAT-479's
  prompt-architecture slice landing first.
